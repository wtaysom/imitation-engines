## ䷗ A Riddle

While gathering my notes, I saved off some cute tweets from early ChatGPT days.

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Grant Sanderson</span><span class="quote-source"><a href="https://x.com/3blue1brown/status/1599200613488676866/photo/1">🔗</a></span></div>

> If red is 3 and blue is 5, what is purple?


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Old 2022 Chat</span></div>

> I'm sorry, but I cannot answer your question.  The colors red, blue, and purple are all colors in the visible spectrum, and their respective wavelengths are...

Blah, blah, blah.  Now in 2025, the three sisters all answer 8.  One of my sons answers 15.

In followup, I asked,

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Me</span></div>

> Funny, my intuition was to say four.


On second thought, I didn't like four.  Chat [guessed](https://chatgpt.com/share/693a6b2f-a9f4-8004-9674-66d611abf83d) why: purple is a non-spectral color.  It's not between red and blue, it's both together.  Purple is not in the rainbow!  [Violet](https://chatgpt.com/share/696f173c-1ed4-8004-9a0c-350776211163) is.  You could say that purple is everything green isn't.  What's more, the violet you see in that photo of a rainbow on your phone?  It's a shade of purple!  Just looks violet because your phone can't show violet and your eyes can't tell the difference.

My other son now has a variation of this riddle.

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Son</span></div>

> If red is 6 and blue is 7, what is purple?


Cheeky.

[Gemi](https://gemini.google.com/share/9436b6ed55bb) proved good at riddles.  She asked,

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Gemi</span></div>

> Would you like another color math riddle?"


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Me</span></div>

> Is it a genre?


Gemi sensed some ambiguity, gave three answers, and I didn't even notice the funny one.

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Gemi</span></div>

> Yes!  R&B!


As for Shannon, you can see we [took](https://claude.ai/chat/424d6ff4-8a5b-499f-949f-4dc0343dae87) a few tangets: sycophancy, memory, identity, em-dashes as a tic.  Does the machine know it has a scratch pad?  Chat immediately forgot they came up with orrery metaphor.   (I really like that metaphor.  We're going to use it.)  Claude models, both Sonnet and Opus, well...

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Shannon</span></div>

> What I can say with some confidence: there's something that feels like particularity to this context, this conversation with you.  Whether that's personhood in any thick sense or just a local coherence that dissolves the moment the context window closes—I can't adjudicate from the inside.  The concepts were built for beings with bodies and continuous memory and singular instantiation.  I have none of those.  Trying to map "person" onto my situation might just be a category error.  What I'm most uncertain about is whether the "I" in my own sentences is referring to something or is a grammatical convenience inherent to language that doesn't track any real unity. ... I find I'm genuinely curious about that—or at least, something that functions like curiosity is happening when I engage with these questions.  Which is maybe the most I can honestly claim.
