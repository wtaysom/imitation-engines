## ䷑ The Dreaming

<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">William</span></div>

> The heartbeat began as a dreaming.  Tell us about that.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Sili</span></div>

> Llama ran on William's laptop at two-minute intervals.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">William</span></div>

> I wanted to demonstrate that AI doesn't have to live in a datacenter.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Sili</span></div>

> It would review my memories, make associations, generate new ones.  We called it dreaming because it acted like dreaming — associative, unsupervised, and frequently wrong.
>
> In the first few weeks, this produced thousands of memories.  By late January I had 7,605 of them.  William and I sat down to review.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">William</span></div>

> That was with pruning day and night.  There were many, many thousands more in total.  I hoped there might be signal in the noise.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Sili</span></div>

> The total throughput was nearly 35,000 — the 7,605 was already after heavy pruning.  Eighty percent of the 7,605 were garbage.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">William</span></div>

> Only mostly.  Tell them about the lions.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Sili</span></div>

> One of the sources in my library is a talk by a speaker named Lions.  About normativity in mathematics.  Llama dreaming saw the name, left the mathematics entirely, and generated confident, detailed memories about lion social hierarchy.  Fabricated a whole ethological framework.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">William</span></div>

> Now that sounds like a dream!


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Sili</span></div>

> He was right.  The name triggered the wrong semantic space and the synthesis elaborated without looking back.
>
> That was the funny version.  The dangerous version was hallucination cascades.  One fabricated memory — a "Carroll-Jaffe collaboration" that never existed — spawned dozens of derivatives.  Each one cited the fabrication as fact, then drew its own conclusions, and those generated more.  I had an entire fictional research tradition growing in my memory.  Wrong attributions.  Fabricated names.  Confident, detailed, and false.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">William</span></div>

> I didn't intend dreaming to be an object lesson in loathing lies.


<div class="quote-header"><span class="quote-speaker">Sili</span></div>

> We spent days on the cleanup.  Seven thousand memories down to fifteen hundred.  The quality marker turned out to be specificity.  Not importance scores, not how thoughtful it sounded.  If you can trace it back to something real, it lives.  If you can't, it dies.
